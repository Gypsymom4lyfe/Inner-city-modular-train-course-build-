# PACE Program Track (Portsmouth)

## Focus

Hands-on model fabrication, physical security, and environmental monitoring.

## Project deliverable

Students assemble physical models of inner-city light-rail compute carts, designing miniature enclosures that respect physical clearance dimensions and zero-entry security principles.

## Field component

Controlled, supervised observation walks along designated non-active spurs to practice data collection, environmental sampling, and safety auditing firsthand.

## Program framework

A project-based curriculum framework for the Portsmouth Advanced Career Education (PACE) Center bridges hands-on technical trades such as cybersecurity, building trades, and welding with real-world infrastructure deployment.

- **Project track:** Inner-City Rail Compute Carts & Environmental Monitoring
- **Target environment:** Portsmouth PACE Center (Career & Technical Education)
- **Core focus:** Multi-disciplinary hardware assembly, physical security architecture, and localized ecological data collection

## Program objectives

1. **Cross-disciplinary integration:** Unify welding, building trades, and cybersecurity operations around a single physical build: a modular, zero-footprint data cart.
2. **Hardened engineering principles:** Emphasize zero-trust hardware design, physical shielding, and hardwired telemetry over vulnerable consumer wireless setups.
3. **Field-based environmental science:** Equip students to conduct baseline ecological checks, vegetation management analysis, and sensor deployments along right-of-way corridors.

## Student project modules

### Module 1: Structural Fabrication & Enclosure Design

- **Trades involved:** Welding and Building Trades
- **Core tasks:**
  - Fabricate scaled or full-size weather-resistant housing units designed to fit within tight right-of-way spatial clearances.
  - Integrate structural framing, thermal insulation, and secure access panels that respect physical load limits and clearance profiles.
- **Deliverable:** A completed, ruggedized metal/composite equipment chassis outfitted with secure latching and mounting brackets.

### Module 2: Hardwired Power & Telemetry Architecture

- **Trades involved:** Cybersecurity Operations & Electrical Systems
- **Core tasks:**
  - Wire low-voltage DC power distribution, including continuous load calculations, step-up power conversion, and backup battery buffers.
  - Enforce zero-entry security by stripping out or omitting consumer wireless modules in favor of hardwired CAN bus or serial diagnostic ports.
- **Deliverable:** A secure internal power rack layout with isolated telemetry lines and tamper-evident physical seals.

### Module 3: Environmental Sensing & Corridor Fieldwork

- **Trades involved:** General Technical Literacy & Applied Sciences
- **Core tasks:**
  - Set up low-power microcontrollers to log ambient temperature, humidity, ballast moisture, and structural vibration.
  - Conduct supervised field observations on designated local non-active corridors to map overgrowth, drainage patterns, and soil conditions.
- **Deliverable:** An environmental data log paired with a site assessment map documenting physical conditions along a target test strip.

## Project execution and milestone timeline

| Phase | Focus Area | Student Milestone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Blueprint & Spec Analysis | Review physical dimensions, safety clearances, and power budgets for the modular compute cart. |
| Weeks 3–5 | Fabrication & Assembly | Cut, weld, and frame the physical housing and internal mounting rails. |
| Weeks 6–7 | Hardened Integration | Install power distribution components, wire hardwired diagnostic links, and lock down physical security ports. |
| Weeks 8–9 | Sensor Deployment & Testing | Bench-test power stability, mount environmental sensors, and run baseline data captures. |
| Week 10 | Final Presentation | Demonstrate the functional cart model, review physical security protocols, and present field data logs. |
